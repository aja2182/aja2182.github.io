About me

I received my Ph.D. in Sociology from Columbia University in 2023 and am currently a postdoctoral fellow in the Department of Population Health at NYU Grossman School of Medicine. I trained as an ethnographer early in my undergraduate career, worked in product development and research in the Bay Area, and then returned to graduate school in New York, where I retrained in poverty governance, comparative welfare and punishment studies, and applied statistical methods. I eventually found my way to public health, where I study how social, organizational, and policy shape access to behavioral healthcare and the often-hidden obstacles that stand between people and care that is, at least nominally, available to them.

My research interests include the social organization of addiction and psychiatric services, with a particular focus on the provision of services to adolescents and young adults, pregnant women, and adults living with ADHD. My work has appeared in Social Science & Medicine, Ethnography, Stigma and Health, and leading journals in harm reduction and addiction medicine, as well as in public media.

I received my B.A. from Bard College in 2014, where I studied sociology and dance. I was born and raised in Brooklyn, New York, and spent much of my late teens hanging out in Union Square and Prospect Park. Today, I spend most of my time contemplating things (you probably do too), logging reps and sets, and, like a good millennial, keeping my top eight friends close.

P.S. I love collaborating with other social and health scientists and providing feedback on working papers, so reach out if you'd like!
