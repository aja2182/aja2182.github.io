About me

I'm a sociologist and postdoctoral fellow in the Department of Population Health at NYU Langone Health. I study the often-hidden obstacles that stand between people and the behavioral healthcare that is, at least nominally, available to them. My current work focuses on young adults and pregnant women navigating substance use treatment and recovery. I am developing an independent research program that asks how organizations shape treatment practices and outcomes for young people with co-occurring ADHD and substance use disorder.

My work combines ethnography and interviews with large-scale administrative data, and has appeared in Social Science & Medicine, Ethnography, Stigma and Health, Journal of Addiction Medicine, Harm Reduction Journal, and Obstetrics & Gynecology.

I was born and raised in Brooklyn, New York. I received my Ph.D. in Sociology from Columbia in 2023 and my B.A. from Bard College. In 2026, I founded Recovery Science, a consultancy that helps recovery residences and treatment programs move science into practice.
