About me

I received my Ph.D. in Sociology from Columbia University in 2023 and am currently a postdoctoral fellow in the Department of Population Health at NYU Grossman School of Medicine. I trained as an ethnographer early in my undergraduate career, worked in product development and research in the Bay Area, and then returned to graduate school in New York, where I retrained in social theory and applied statistical methods. I eventually found my way to public health, where I study how social, organizational, and policy arrangements shape access to behavioral healthcare and the often-hidden obstacles that stand between people and care that is, at least nominally, available to them.

My research and teaching interests include the social organization of addiction and psychiatric services, with a particular focus on adolescents and young adults, pregnant women, and adults living with ADHD. My work has appeared in Social Science & Medicine, Ethnography, Stigma and Health, and leading journals in addiction medicine, as well as in public media. I received my B.A. from Bard College in 2014, where I studied sociology and dance. I was born and raised in Brooklyn, New York, and spent much of my late teens hanging out in Union Square and Prospect Park.

I spend most of my time contemplating things (you probably do too), logging reps and sets, and, like a good millennial, keeping my top eight friends close.

Research Interests

My current work focuses on three key areas of research: (1) identifying facilitators and barriers to appealing and effective treatment across publicly and commercially insured patients, organizations, and policy contexts, (2) detailing the motivation and meaning of treatment and recovery among young adult patients and their providers, and (3) assessing the clinical course, treatment, and outcomes of adolescents and young adults with comorbid ADHD and substance use disorder.
