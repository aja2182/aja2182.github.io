About me

I'm a sociologist who studies how people find their way into addiction and mental health care, and the often-hidden obstacles that stand between them and care that is, at least nominally, available. I'm currently a postdoctoral fellow in the Department of Population Health at NYU Grossman School of Medicine.

My path here wasn't exactly direct. I trained as an ethnographer early in my undergraduate career, worked in product development and research in the Bay Area, and then returned to New York for a Ph.D. in Sociology at Columbia (2023). There I retrained in the sociology of medicine, organizational sociology, and welfare and punishment using historical, comparative, and statistical methods, and eventually found my way to public health.

These days, most of my research focuses on young adults in addiction treatment: how they move through the treatment system, and why conditions like ADHD so often go unnoticed and untreated along the way. My work has appeared in Social Science & Medicine, Ethnography, Stigma and Health, and leading journals in harm reduction and addiction medicine, as well as in public media.

More recently, I started Recovery Sciences, a consultancy where I get to put some of that research to work. I help recovery residences and treatment programs build out peer support, case management, and ways of tracking whether any of it is actually helping.

I received my B.A. from Bard College in 2014, where I studied sociology and dance. I was born and raised in Brooklyn and spent much of my late teens hanging out in Union Square and Prospect Park. Today, I spend most of my time contemplating things (you probably do too), logging reps and sets, and keeping my closest friends closer.

P.S. I love collaborating with other social and health scientists and giving feedback on working papers, so reach out!
