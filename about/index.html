About me

I'm a sociologist and postdoctoral fellow in the Health Evaluation and Analytics (HEAL) Lab in the Department of Population Health at NYU Grossman School of Medicine. I study the often-hidden obstacles that stand between people and the behavioral healthcare that is, at least nominally, available to them. My current work focuses on the experience and outcomes of young adults (ages 18–25) in substance use treatment, a population whose needs are poorly served by treatment systems designed for older adults. I am developing an independent research program aimed at evaluating and improving treatment practices and outcomes among young people with co-occurring ADHD and substance use disorder.

I combine ethnography and interviews with large-scale administrative data, including linked New York State treatment registry and Medicaid claims data developed in partnership with state agencies. My work has appeared in Social Science & Medicine, Ethnography, Stigma and Health, Journal of Addiction Medicine, Harm Reduction Journal, and Obstetrics & Gynecology.

I received my Ph.D. in Sociology from Columbia in 2023 and my B.A. from Bard College. Before graduate school, I worked in design and user experience research at Google, Adobe, and InVision.

In 2026, I founded Recovery Sciences, a consultancy that helps recovery residences and treatment programs move science into practice.

I was born and raised in Brooklyn. Reach out if you'd like to collaborate or swap working papers.
